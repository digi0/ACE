## 1. One-paragraph brand brief

> ACE is the navigation layer for college. Universities are enormous, badly-indexed
> institutions where every answer lives in a different office, on a different website,
> behind different hours — and students are handed a login and told to figure it out.
> ACE is one place that knows your college: ask it anything, any hour, academic or not,
> and get a real answer with a citation. Course planning is the hardest thing it does,
> not the only thing. Six words: **college, sorted.**

## 2. Product & category

- **Name:** ACE (written `ace.` in the logo — lowercase, with a period).
- **Category:** AI academic advisor / student navigation app. Not a chatbot, not a
  "student success platform."
- **What it does:** answers questions about the student's specific college — courses,
  prerequisites, degree requirements, deadlines, registration, plus the non-academic
  half (dining, housing, financial aid, orgs, campus services). Students can upload a
  degree audit for personalised analysis.
- **How answers are grounded:** structured university data — **749 degree programs,
  9,439 course records, 46 handbook policy rules** — with **page-level citations**.
- **Surface:** web app at acecollege.app, mobile-friendly, installable (PWA).
- **Origin:** built at Penn State (started with CS and Data Science), now covers every
  major. `[CONFIRM]` which campuses are live today.

## 3. Who it's for

**Primary:** undergraduates, any major, **mid-degree** — sophomores and juniors who have
already made a choice they're unsure about. Not the freshman with a clean slate; the one
with a question that has a wrong answer.

**Second audience, acquired free:** parents. They never install anything — they receive a
screenshot. Every answer should survive being forwarded to whoever pays tuition.

**Not the buyer, and not the enemy:** advising offices. We are not a procurement sale and
we never campaign against advisors. We are the hours they don't work.

**Demographics:** 18–23, US, smartphone-first, distrusts institutional voice, trusts peer
voice, active on TikTok/IG/Reddit/group chats.

---

## 4. The problem, in the customer's words

The real pain is not "I can't find a course." It's a low, constant dread:
*"I think I'm behind, I'm not sure, and everyone else seems to have it figured out."*

Three components, all usable as ad angles:
1. **Uncertainty** — the rules exist but are scattered across a 40-page handbook, a
   bulletin site, and an advisor with a three-week queue.
2. **Shame** — asking feels like admitting you should already know. This is why students
   Google at 2am instead of emailing at 2pm.
3. **Comparison** — the belief everyone else has a plan. They don't; they have the same
   tabs open.

**Timing:** demand is nocturnal and calendar-spiked — the week registration opens, the
week before drop deadlines, the night before an advising appointment, the 48 hours after
grades post.

---

## 5. USPs, ranked

1. **It cites its source.** Page-level citation to the actual handbook or bulletin. This
   is the difference between a chatbot and an advisor, and it's the lead claim.
2. **It knows *your* college, not college in general.** Real program and course data, not
   a generic model answering from memory.
3. **It's awake.** 2am, every night, no queue, no appointment.
4. **It covers everything, not just courses.** Dining, housing, aid, orgs, services —
   the whole institution.
5. **Every major.** 749 programs, not just the popular ones.
6. **No shame tax.** You can ask it the thing you're embarrassed to ask a person.

- Real product transcripts: e.g. a student asking *"can I still graduate by spring '27

  Screenshots of real exchanges are our strongest proof asset.

**`[NONE YET]` — do not let Parker invent these:**
- Customer testimonials or quotes.
- User counts, "trusted by X students," ratings.
- Outcome statistics (graduation rates, time saved, GPA change).
- Case studies, before/after results, revenue claims.

Our proof strategy is **transcripts, not a logo wall** — publish the hardest real
questions with citations, *including cases where ACE was corrected*. Falsifiable proof
beats testimonial proof for this audience.

## 7. Voice — how to write as ACE

**Register:** the friend who happens to have read the handbook. Peer, never registrar.

**Rules:**
- **Lowercase.** Capitals read institutional.
- **Lead with the verdict.** "yes." then the reason. Never make an anxious person read a
  paragraph to find out if they're okay.
- **Mistakes are routes, not failures.** "failed it? rerouted." The error is a fact about
  the map, not about them.
- **Never take the credit.** "you're fine" — not "we fixed it." Agency stays with the student.
- **No exclamation marks.** Confidence doesn't shout; enthusiasm reads as sales.
- **Short sentences.** One idea each.
- **Sentences end in a period, and the period is the brand.** In visual assets, the final
  period is emerald.

**Test for any line:** could a sophomore text this to their roommate without irony? If
not, rewrite it.

**Sample lines, in voice:**
> college, sorted. · ask your college anything. · your 2am question. answered. ·
> failed it? rerouted. · 12 credits left. you're fine. · 0 warnings. ·
> still answering. (over a 02:46 timestamp)

**Words we use:** advisor · sorted · handled · rerouted · the plan · the route ·
cited to page 14 · every major · you're fine

**Words we never use:** chatbot · assistant · bot · AI-powered · powered by AI ·
student success platform · empower · revolutionize · transform · optimize · streamline ·
unlock · journey · roadmap · pathway · game-changer · "Built for X"

## 8. Compliance & guardrails — THE CRITICAL FIELD

Paste this verbatim into Parker's compliance/restrictions field. Advising is a domain
where a confident wrong claim costs a student a semester.

```
HARD RULES FOR ALL ACE CREATIVE:

1. Never promise an outcome. No "graduate on time, guaranteed," no "never miss a
   requirement again," no implied guarantee of academic results. We show the route;
   the student walks it and the university awards the degree.

2. Never imply university affiliation or endorsement. ACE is not affiliated with,
   endorsed by, or speaking for Penn State or any institution. No university logos,
   colours, seals, mascots, campus trademarks, or implied partnership.

3. Never claim to replace academic advisors. Petitions, substitutions and exceptions
   are human decisions. Position as "the advisor who's awake," never as a replacement.

4. Never joke about or dramatise uploaded student documents. Degree audits are
   sensitive academic records. No real names, no real student data in creative.

5. Never manufacture urgency or scarcity. No countdowns, no "limited spots," no
   fake deadlines. Real academic deadlines are urgent enough, and the audience is
   already anxious. This is the one thing this brand cannot survive doing.

6. No fabricated proof. No invented testimonials, user counts, ratings, or statistics.
   If we don't have the number, the creative doesn't use a number.

7. No shaming or fear-based hooks. Never "you're already behind," never "your friends
   are ahead of you." We reduce anxiety; we do not sell with it.

8. No exaggerated AI claims. Don't lead with the model. The citation is the value.
```

## 10. Competitors & landscape

We don't compete with a product so much as with **the current mess**:

| Alternative | Why students use it | Where it fails |
|---|---|---|
| The advisor | Authoritative, can approve exceptions | Three-week queue, business hours, one appointment a term |
| University websites & handbooks | It's the actual source | Documents, not answers; scattered; unreadable |
| Reddit / group chats / upperclassmen | Fast, peer voice, covers everything | No accountability, often wrong, no citation |
| Generic AI chatbots | Instant, free | Doesn't know *your* college's rules; no citation; confidently wrong |
| Degree-audit tools in the SIS | Official | Built for administrators, not students |

**The gap we own: breadth plus a citation.** Advisors have authority without hours.
Reddit has breadth without accountability. We have both.

## 12. Channels & metrics

**Channels, in priority order:** campus bottom-up (RAs, orientation leaders, org
officers, course group chats) · organic short-form (TikTok/Reels/IG) · programmatic
course-code content/SEO over 9,439 courses · the 1–3am window · die-cut sticker seeding.

**Explicitly not:** university procurement, "official partnership" announcements,
anything positioning us as an institutional product.

**Metrics we judge creative against** (give Parker these, not ROAS):
1. **Categories per student per term** — the north star. 1 = we built a course tool,
   3+ = we built the layer.
2. **Resolution rate** — sessions ending in an answer or routed next step.
3. **Off-peak weekly actives** — usage in boring weeks, the honest test of habit.
4. **Non-academic share of questions** — proves breadth without abandoning the moat.
5. **Shares per resolved plan** — how often an answer leaves as a screenshot.

Refuse: total messages, session length, DAU without resolution context, and any
retention number measured only around registration windows.

## 13. On-brand creative angles (starter set for Parker)

1. **The 2am timestamp.** "still answering." over 02:46. Owns the hour nobody markets in.
2. **The forwarded screenshot.** A plan a student sends their mum. Speaks to both audiences.
3. **The wrong-answer receipt.** ACE citing page 14 — proof by transcript.
4. **"ask your college anything."** Rapid-fire mixed-register questions: prereqs, dining
   hours, how to join a club in October, what a hold is.
5. **The tab graveyard.** Fourteen university tabs open vs one input box.
6. **Week one.** Orientation questions nobody wants to ask a human.
7. **Rerouted.** Failed a class → still fine → here's the path. Shame-free framing.

**Banned angles:** "don't be that student," anything implying the viewer is behind,
countdown/urgency, fake DMs or testimonials, university-branded visuals, "AI will do your
degree for you," anything that mocks students for not knowing.

## 14. Visual identity (for any generated concept)

- **Mark:** lowercase `a` leaning −11°, with a level emerald period. Wordmark `ace.` is
  upright. Assets in `brand/logo/`; rules in `brand/logo-standards.pdf`.
- **Palette:** six greys, no hue — ground `#F6F6F6`, card `#FFFFFF`, rule `#E4E4E4`,
  ghost `#B2B2B2`, mute `#666666`, ink `#0A0A0A` — plus **one accent, emerald `#00875A`**.
- **The colour law:** emerald appears **once per view, and it is always the period** —
  the send button, the due date, the destination, the done. A second emerald element in a
  frame is wrong.
- **Type:** lowercase sans for speech, monospace for numbers only (zero-padded: `087/120`,
  `WEEK 04`). Text never exceeds 28px; the wordmark carries scale.
- **No:** gradients, drop shadows, stock photography, university imagery, confetti,
  exclamation marks, emoji in brand voice.

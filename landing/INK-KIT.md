# ace. — the ink kit

Everything Raghav draws for the site, how to draw it, and how to hand it over.
First-time-drawing friendly. **You do not need to be good at this** — the whole
point of a zine line is that it isn't polished. A slightly wrong circle is the
asset. A perfect one is worthless.

---

## 0. THE LAW — read before anything else

**The structure carries the site. Ink is punctuation.**

The site is a site: grid, type, space, real product UI. Clean and confident. Ink
never *builds* anything — it annotates. So it takes the same law as the emerald:

> **If two ink marks are visible in one viewport, one of them is wrong.**

Scope was cut from 23 drawings to **4** on 2026-08-01, at Raghav's call: "too many
of such objects will end up creating a chaos on our website — a website has to at
the end of the day look like a website." He was right. Concentrated ink reads as
craft; sprinkled ink reads as a scrapbook, and it would have cost the investor
credibility the page also has to carry.

### The one signature moment

Ink concentrates into a single composition: **a marked-up degree audit.** A friend
who's a year ahead takes the document you can't read and annotates it — circles
the course blocking you, arrows what to do about it, ticks what's already cleared.

That is the wedge, the positioning made literal, and the screenshot. Everywhere
else on the page, ink appears once or not at all.

## 1. Setup — do this once

### App — either route works

**Freeform (free, built in)** — CHOSEN. Same PencilKit engine as Apple Notes.
See §1b for the Freeform-specific setup; the rest of this file applies unchanged.

**Procreate (~$13)** — more control (brush size slider, streamline, transparent
export). Only worth it if the Freeform line turns out to be limiting.

*(Skip Concepts/Illustrator. Vector apps sound better but they fight you into
neatness, which is the exact opposite of what this style needs.)*

### 1b. Freeform setup

1. New board. **Turn the grid off** — it defaults to a dot grid and it will show
   up in the export. The toggle is in the board's view options.
2. **Pen tool, largest size, black.** Freeform gives preset sizes rather than a
   slider, so take the thickest. Fountain Pen instead if you want taper.
3. No streamline control here — **drawing fast does the same job.**
4. **Shape-snapping is aggressive in PencilKit.** Holding the Pencil still at the
   end of a stroke corrects it into a perfect shape and ruins the mark. Lift
   immediately, every time.
5. Draw each mark **large** — roughly a hand's width on screen — and leave a
   clear gap between them. Several per board is fine; they get cut apart later.
6. **Share → Export as PDF.** Not a screenshot. PDF keeps full fidelity and may
   preserve real vector paths, which helps the draw-on-scroll animation.

Freeform can't export transparency, so everything arrives on white. That's fine —
the paper gets thresholded out on the receiving end. It was a Procreate
convenience, not a requirement.

### Canvas — Procreate only (Freeform's canvas is infinite; just draw big)

Gallery → **+** → the small folder icon (Custom canvas) →

| setting | value |
|---|---|
| Width | **2048 px** |
| Height | **2048 px** |
| DPI | **300** |
| Colour profile | **sRGB** |

Save it as a preset called `ink`. Use it for everything except the two big
drawings in §4, which get **3000 × 2200**.

### Brush

**Inking → Studio Pen.**

Set the size so the line is **clearly chunky when the whole canvas fits your
screen**. If it looks thin at full view, it's too thin — roughly 15–20% on the
size slider. Colour: pure black.

One adjustment worth making: tap the brush twice → **Stabilization → Streamline
→ about 20%**. That takes out hand jitter without killing the character. Above
40% it starts looking machine-drawn.

### Two settings that will otherwise ruin your drawings

1. **QuickShape.** If you hold the Pencil still at the end of a stroke, Procreate
   snaps your line into a perfect shape. That destroys the entire aesthetic.
   **Lift the Pencil immediately when you finish a stroke.**
2. **Background layer.** Before exporting, open the Layers panel and **untick the
   checkbox next to "Background colour"** at the bottom. Your PNG then exports
   with a transparent background and I don't have to cut the paper out.

## 2. How to draw — the only technique note that matters

**One pass. Draw fast. Don't go over a line twice.**

Speed is what makes a line look confident. Slow careful lines look traced, and
tracing is exactly what reads as fake. If a stroke comes out wrong, undo the
whole thing and redo it in one go rather than patching it.

Zoom out so the whole shape fits on screen and draw it with your **arm**, not
your fingers. Wrist-drawing produces the tight, hesitant line we're trying to
avoid.

Wobble is good. Overshoot is good. Lines that don't quite meet are good.

## 3. TIER 1 — the marginalia · draw these first

Fifteen marks, ten seconds each. **These do more for the site than the big
drawings do** — they get scattered across every screen, circling a course code,
arrowing a question to its answer, ticking off a requirement. They're what makes
the whole thing feel hand-touched.

Each on its own 2048² canvas. Draw it **large** — filling most of the canvas.

| # | file | what it is | how to make it read as hand-drawn |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | `circle.png` | a circle drawn around something | Start around 10 o'clock, go all the way round in one motion, and **overshoot past your start by about 15% so the ends cross**. The crossing is the whole trick. Slightly egg-shaped, not round. |
| 02 | `circle-2.png` | a second, different circle | Same again but wonkier and wider. I'll alternate them so the site never repeats the same mark twice in a view. |
| 03 | `arrow-straight.png` | → | Shaft first. Then the head as a **separate V**, two strokes, slightly uneven. Never one continuous stroke — that's what makes arrows look like clip art. |
| 04 | `arrow-curve.png` | a curving arrow, left to right | A shallow arc, then the same separate V at the end. |
| 05 | `arrow-hook.png` | an arrow that bends down sharply | Like you're pointing from a heading down to a note beneath it. |
| 06 | `underline.png` | a rough underline | One fast stroke. Should taper and not be level. |
| 07 | `underline-2.png` | a double underline | Two strokes, not parallel, second shorter than the first. |
| 08 | `check.png` | ✓ | Two strokes. Short down-stroke, long up-stroke. Let the long arm overshoot. |
| 09 | `cross.png` | ✗ | Two strokes crossing. Don't centre the crossing point. |
| 10 | `strike.png` | scribbled-out text | A horizontal zigzag, 5–6 hard back-and-forths, as if you scratched out a word. |
| 11 | `box.png` | a hand-drawn rectangle | Four strokes, corners overshooting past each other. Never close it neatly. |
| 12 | `bracket.png` | a tall `[` | For bracketing a group of lines. One stroke if you can. |
| 13 | `star.png` | an asterisk / star | Three crossing strokes, uneven lengths. |
| 14 | `question.png` | a big `?` | Drawn, not typed. Fat and confident. |
| 15 | `arrow-loop.png` | a looping arrow that curls back on itself | The "come back to this" mark. |

## 5. TIER 3 — the two real drawings · 3000 × 2200 canvas

Take your time on these two. They carry actual weight.

### `audit.png` — the degree audit printout

**This is the most important drawing in the set.** It's the wedge object: the
document nobody can read, which ACE reads for you.

- A stack seen at a slight three-quarter angle, not flat-on.
- The top page as a wonky rectangle. Then **3–4 short offset lines along the left
  and bottom edges** to imply the pages underneath. That's all "thickness" needs.
- A staple top-left: two short parallel ticks.
- Inside, **10–14 horizontal lines of varying length** standing in for text.
  Never let them touch the page edges. Vary the gaps — a couple of tight
  clusters, one bigger gap, like real section breaks.
- Optional and good: one line noticeably shorter, like a heading.

Don't draw readable words. Lines only.

### `laptop.png` — an open laptop

- Three-quarter view, opened maybe 100°.
- Screen as a parallelogram, keyboard base as a shallower parallelogram.
- **Leave the screen empty.** I'll put the interface in with type — that keeps it
  crisp and lets it animate.
- One line for the trackpad, a few ticks for keys. Don't draw every key.

## 6. Export & handover

**Freeform:** Share → **Export as PDF**. One PDF can hold several marks as long
as they're well spaced. Name it for what's on it (`marks-batch-1.pdf`).

**Procreate:** Layers panel → untick **"Background colour"** → Wrench (Actions) →
Share → **PNG**, named as in the tables above.

Either way, drop the files into **`landing/public/ink/`**.

**Never JPG.** No transparency, and it smears the line edges — which is exactly
the detail that has to survive.

## 7. Start with three

Don't draw all twenty-three. Draw:

1. `circle.png`
2. `arrow-straight.png`
3. `audit.png`

Send those and I'll run the full pipeline on them — knock them into the palette,
recolour one emerald, and animate the audit so it draws itself on scroll. You'll
see exactly what your line looks like on the real site before you commit an
afternoon to the rest.

Same rule as everything else here: never commit a batch to an unproven look.

## 8. What I do on my end

- Recolour: your black line becomes any grey in the palette, or emerald where a
  mark needs to mean *resolved*.
- Riso treatment: halftone and a slight ink misregistration over the top, so
  everything reads as printed rather than placed.
- Animation: the marks fade and scale in on scroll; the audit and laptop get
  traced into SVG paths so their **lines draw themselves** as you scroll past.
  That tracing is my work, not yours — you just draw it once.
